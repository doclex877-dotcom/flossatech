<h2 id="the-stc-catch">The catch hiding in every wattage rating: STC</h2>

Nearly every solar charger's wattage rating — 15W, 25W, 30W — is measured
under **Standard Test Conditions (STC)**: 1,000 watts per square meter of
light intensity, a 25°C cell temperature, and the panel aimed directly
at the light source. That's a real, standardized measurement, not a
fabricated number — but it describes a laboratory best case that real
outdoor conditions rarely match exactly. A panel rated 30W under STC
typically delivers somewhere in the **18–24W range in strong midday
summer sun**, and as little as **10–25% of its rated output under light
cloud cover**.

<h2 id="the-loss-chain">The full loss chain, from sunlight to phone</h2>

Solar phone charging loses energy at four separate, cumulative stages
between sunlight hitting the panel and power reaching your phone's
battery:

1. **Panel conversion:** photovoltaic cells convert roughly **20–25%**
   of incoming sunlight into electricity (for monocrystalline cells —
   see below for how this varies by cell type).
2. **Charge controller:** the circuit regulating voltage and current for
   safe charging loses roughly **5–10%**.
3. **Battery charging:** charging a lithium-ion/lithium-polymer cell is
   roughly **90% efficient**; the rest is lost as heat.
4. **Output stage:** the boost converter raising battery voltage to
   5V (or higher, for fast charging) over USB loses another
   **10–15%** — the same voltage-conversion loss covered in our power
   bank capacity guide.

Stacked together, of every 100 units of solar energy hitting the panel,
roughly **15–18 units** actually reach your phone.

<h2 id="cell-type">Why cell type changes everything</h2>

Not all solar cells convert sunlight at the same rate, and the
difference is large enough to be the single biggest lever in a panel's
real-world performance:

| Cell type | Typical efficiency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monocrystalline silicon | ~20–25% | Standard for portable chargers; performs better in overcast or partial shade |
| Polycrystalline | ~15–18% | Needs a larger panel for the same output as monocrystalline |
| Thin-film | Rarely above ~12% | Flexible and lightweight, but meaningfully less efficient — a trade-off worth making only if minimizing weight matters more than charging speed |

<Callout kind="verified" title="A real example of efficiency mattering more than size">
One tested 25W foldable panel using N-type monocrystalline cells reached
a measured **25.4% efficiency** — high enough that reviewers found it
outperformed larger, higher-wattage-rated panels using lower-efficiency
cells. Efficiency, not the headline wattage number, determined which
panel actually charged faster in the same real sunlight.
</Callout>

<h2 id="real-numbers">Real panels, real measured output</h2>

Independent real-world testing of specific rated panels shows the STC-to-
reality gap concretely:

| Panel rating (STC) | Measured real-world output (strong midday sun) |
|---|---|
| 30W | 22–24W |
| 25W (high-efficiency monocrystalline) | ~20W across both USB ports |
| 10W | ~6–7W average |

That's a meaningfully better ratio for the higher-efficiency panels than
for cheaper, lower-efficiency ones at a similar rated wattage — which is
exactly why two "25W" panels from different manufacturers can perform
noticeably differently in the same sunlight.

<h2 id="matching-panel-to-need">Matching panel size to what you actually need</h2>

A typical smartphone draws roughly **5–10W** while charging (briefly more
during early fast-charge negotiation). Working backward from the loss
chain above:

- **A 10W-rated panel** delivering ~6–7W real output can trickle-charge a
  phone but isn't fast enough for full daily replenishment on its own.
- **A 15W-rated panel** in good direct sun can charge a phone at close to
  a standard wall-charger's pace — roughly 1–2 hours for a full charge.
- **A 25–30W-rated panel** is worth the extra size and weight mainly if
  you also want to recharge a power bank simultaneously, or charge
  multiple devices across a multi-day trip.

<h2 id="reading-a-listing">How to read a solar charger listing</h2>

1. **Treat the headline wattage as an STC lab figure**, not a guaranteed
   field result — expect roughly 60–80% of it in strong direct sun, and
   considerably less under cloud cover.
2. **Check the cell type** (monocrystalline, polycrystalline, thin-film)
   — it predicts real performance better than wattage alone, especially
   comparing two similarly rated panels.
3. **Look for a stated USB output voltage/amperage** (e.g., "USB-C PD
   18W" or "5V/3A") rather than trusting the panel's peak wattage rating
   alone — the port's actual negotiated output is what your phone
   receives.
4. **Pair a solar panel with a power bank for realistic daily use**,
   rather than expecting direct-to-phone charging to fully replace wall
   charging — charging the bank during peak sun and drawing from it
   later avoids the slowdowns caused by clouds, shade, and a moving sun
   angle.

<h2 id="bottom-line">Bottom line</h2>

A solar charger's wattage rating is a real, standardized lab measurement
— just one taken under conditions your backyard or trailhead will rarely
match exactly. Between STC-to-real-sun losses and the same
voltage-conversion losses that affect every USB power bank, expect
somewhere around 60–80% of the rated wattage in genuinely strong direct
sun, and cell type (not the headline wattage number) as the better
predictor of which specific panel will actually perform well against
another one rated similarly.
